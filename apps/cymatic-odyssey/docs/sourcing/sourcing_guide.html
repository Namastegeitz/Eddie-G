Idea: engrave the 600-cell or E8 point cloud inside a crystal cube. Placed in the beam, every engraved point scatters its own spark of light. Ask the maker whether they can work from a 3D point file.

What to send a custom maker
  1. The shape: an SVG from downloads/gobos_*.zip or an outline from the catalogue.
  2. Size and thickness, and the tolerance you need (a flat, even bottom matters most).
  3. Material: borosilicate 3.3 for water vessels; optical or float glass for plates.
  4. Edge finish (seamed, polished) and whether it needs tempering.
  5. Quantity, budget and deadline. Ask for a quote and a lead time.

Printing for clarity

  • Dry the spool first (PETG soaks up moisture).
  • 100% infill, slower and a little hotter, wide extrusion, so the layers fuse with no air gaps.
  • Flat on the bed, few walls; sand and clear-coat for glass-like faces.
  • Single-wall vase mode makes even diffusers.

The rainbow trick

  • Print a gobo in clear PETG.
  • Sandwich it between two polariser sheets in the beam.
  • Stress frozen in by printing shows as rainbow colours (photoelasticity).
  • Rotate one polariser and every colour shifts.

Hardware for effects

  • Hardened-steel nozzle for glow, glitter and metal-filled filament.
  • 0.6 mm nozzle if you print a lot of galaxy or glitter.
  • A 365-395 nm UV LED channel to charge glow and trigger UV-shift and neon.

Keep out of the light path

  • Glitter, silk and metal fill: they scatter and block light.
  • Thermochromic near the LED heatsink.
  • PLA in water for weeks: use PETG for anything wet.

Brand claims about clarity, glow strength and colour-change come from manufacturer, retailer and review pages. Test a small print from each spool before committing to a large part.
