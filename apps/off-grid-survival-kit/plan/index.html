Trip Plan & Turnaround

Before you go, leave a trip plan with two people who will notice if you don't come back: where you're going, when you expect to be back, and the time they must call for help. Most lost people are found quickly when someone knows the plan and they stay put.

Everything stays on this phone until you copy, share or print it. Time estimates are rough minimums — terrain, weather, load and fitness all slow you down. In an emergency with any signal, call 911 (US/Canada) or 112 (Europe).

Leave a trip plan

  1. Fill this in before you leave. It saves on this phone as you type.
  2. Send or give it to two people who are not on the trip and will notice if you're not back.
  3. Agree the call-for-help time. If you're not back by then, they call — they don't wait.
  4. Stick to the plan. If it changes, tell them. When you're back, tell them straight away.

      Naismith's rule is a minimum
      
      It assumes a fit walker on a good path in good weather with a light pack and no rests. Rough or steep ground, snow, river crossings, heat, a heavy pack, tired or injured people and getting lost all add time. Add breaks, and plan to finish well before dark.

      Golden rules
      
    
      Turn around on time
      
      When your turnaround time comes, turn around — no matter how close the goal is. Getting back is the only part of the trip that isn't optional. Turn back early for bad weather, an injury, someone struggling, or if you're behind at a checkpoint.
      
    
      Lost? Stay put
      
        
        
      1. Stop. Sit down, drink, eat something. Don't keep walking to “find the trail”.
        2. 
        
      2. Stay where you are — “hug a tree”. Searchers start from your trip plan; moving makes you harder to find.
        3. 
        
      3. Get warm and dry. Shelter from wind and wet, insulate yourself from the ground.
        4. 
        
      4. Be seen and heard. Bright clothing in the open, 3 whistle blasts, a mirror or light at aircraft. See Whistle and SOS light.
        5. 
      
      
      
      Move only if you must for safety (fire, flood, no shelter) — and then leave a note or mark showing which way you went.
      
    
      Plans change
      
      If you change route or will be late, tell your contacts if you can. If you can't reach them, stick to the plan you left.
      
  
      

  
      Time estimates use Naismith's rule (W. W. Naismith, 1892): 1 hour per 5 km (3 mi) on the flat plus 1 hour per 600 m (2,000 ft) of ascent. Sunset and civil dusk (“last light”, sun 6° below the horizon) are calculated on this phone for the chosen location; valleys, forest and cloud make it dark earlier. Trip-plan advice follows common search-and-rescue guidance. Nothing on this page is sent anywhere.
